The redesign was guided by feedback, user testing, and insights from professionals in the field to create a stronger experience for employers.

Problem

Earlier versions of my portfolio lacked a polished, professional feel. The layout, navigation, and project selection didn’t effectively showcase the depth of my skills or clearly communicate my capabilities. As my experience grew, the site quickly became outdated and no longer reflected the breadth of my work or the value I could offer employers.

What I changed

  • Rebuilt structure and visual system for a cleaner, more professional feel.
  • Cut projects that weren’t useful — weak proof, wrong skill signal, or not a fair look at my work.
  • Rewrote cases around ownership, skills, and results so recruiters can scan without a long scroll.
  • Developed the entire portfolio from scratch using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

How I Get Feedback

  • Gather feedback from friends, coworkers, industry professionals, and potential users to identify usability issues and areas for improvement.
  • Apply user-centered design principles by observing how people navigate the site, collecting feedback, and iterating based on their experiences.
  • Develop in iterations—from my freshman-year Adobe portfolio to previous versions and this latest rebuild—using each round of testing to refine the experience.
  • Use feedback to make design decisions: if a project doesn’t clearly demonstrate my skills, impact, or value, it doesn’t make the final portfolio.

Result

A third iteration that better represents my skills, experience, and the type of work I want to do. The portfolio is easier to navigate, showcases stronger project evidence, and reflects an iterative, user-centered design process driven by feedback and continuous improvement. Earlier work remains available in the archive for context.
